Good notes separate what happened from what happens next. EDTRDocs works well for meeting summaries, personal research and quick drafts that may later become a document.

## Start with a repeatable structure

Put the topic and date at the top. Create three headings: **Context**, **Decisions** and **Next steps**. During a meeting, capture rough points under the appropriate heading instead of polishing every sentence.

Type `/` to insert headings or lists. Afterward, turn fragmented notes into short sentences while the discussion is fresh. The [keyboard shortcut guide](/guides/wordpad-keyboard-shortcuts) helps with this cleanup.

## Give action items an owner

For a few actions, a bullet list is enough. For a longer list, create a [table](/guides/insert-table-online-editor) with task, owner and due date. Keep decisions separate from suggestions so a reader can tell what was agreed.

Rename the document with a topic and date before starting another meeting. A recognizable title makes it easier to return to the right document.

## Return to the same notes

Guest documents save in the current browser after edits. They are not saved on every individual keystroke, and clearing browser data can remove them. An optional account adds document sync across devices; it does not create a live shared meeting workspace.

Export important notes as a [DOCX](/guides/export-to-word-docx-online) or another suitable format. On a shared device, closing the tab does not remove the saved guest copy. For short unformatted notes, see the [online notepad guide](/guides/online-notepad).
