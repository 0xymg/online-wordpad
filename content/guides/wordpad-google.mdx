“WordPad Google” can mean finding WordPad through Google Search, opening a WordPad-like editor in Chrome, or comparing it with Google Docs. Those are three different tasks.

## If you want to start writing

You can go directly to [EDTRpad](/pad) in your browser. It is an independent editor on wordpad.info, not a Google product or a Microsoft-hosted version of WordPad.

The [first-document guide](/guides/how-to-use-wordpad-online) covers headings, lists, saving and export. You do not need a Google account for guest writing.

## If you mean Google Docs

Google Docs emphasizes shared documents and collaboration. EDTRpad is suited to individual writing and importing or exporting files. Optional account sync is not live coauthoring.

Read the [Google Docs alternative guide](/guides/google-docs-alternative) to compare those workflows. Moving a document between services does not migrate sharing permissions or review history automatically.

## If you have a file in Drive

Download a compatible DOCX copy, then choose **File → Open** in EDTRpad. Review the result before editing because conversion can change layout. Exporting creates another copy; it does not update the original Drive file.

Follow the [DOCX opening guide](/guides/open-docx-online) for a full checklist. On a Chromebook, also read the [device guide](/guides/wordpad-for-chromebook), especially if a school policy controls downloads or clears local browser storage.
